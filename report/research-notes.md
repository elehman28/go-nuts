## Statistics
- ~10.8% of US adults have convincing food allergy; 19% self-report; peanut 1.8%, tree nut 1.2%; 51.1% had a severe reaction; 38.3% lifetime ED visit, 8.6% ED visit in past 12 months. Gupta et al., "Prevalence and Severity of Food Allergies Among US Adults", JAMA Network Open, 2019 - https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2720064 (fetched)
- ~7.6% of US children have current food allergy; peanut 2.2%, tree nut 1.2%; 42.3% of allergic children had a severe reaction. Gupta et al., Pediatrics, Nov 2018 (figures via search summary; abstract not fetched) - https://nemours.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-public-health-impact-of-parent-reported-childhood-food-allerg/ ; press: https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2018/november/kids-food-allergies-er
- US Peanut and Tree Nut Allergy Registry survey: 13.7% of respondents' reactions linked to restaurants/food establishments; peanut 67%, tree nut 24%; only 45% told staff about allergy; in ~half of those the allergen was hidden (sauces/dressings); 22% likely shared equipment contamination. FARE registry: dining out 2nd most common reaction place (21%) after home (44%). Rotella & Oriel, "Accidental Reactions to Foods: Frequency, Causes, and Severity", Current Treatment Options in Allergy, 2022 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9282141/ (fetched)
- Fatal reactions: US registry 18 of 63 (28%) fatalities tied to food establishments; UK 16 of 48 (33%). Same Rotella & Oriel 2022 review - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9282141/ . Also AAAAI 2020 work group (28%, 18/63; 34% of respondents in a 2007 survey had a restaurant reaction) - https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/Media-Library-PDFs/Allergist Resources/Statements and Practice Parameters/Jan-2020-Food-Allergy-in-Restaurants.pdf (from search summary). CDC MMWR says nearly half of fatal reactions over 13 years came from restaurant/food service food (below). Ontario study: ~14% of anaphylaxis deaths 1986-2011 after restaurant/fast food/food court meals (secondhand via Reuters-type report, https://www.physiciansweekly.com/?p=92982; NOT CONFIRMED). Range is ~14% to ~50% depending on source; do not state one number without its source.
- Staff training: of 278 restaurants in 6 US sites, only 44.4% of managers, 40.8% of food workers, 33.3% of servers reported food allergy training; ~1/4 of managers had no ingredient lists; few had separate allergen-free equipment. Radke et al., "Restaurant Food Allergy Practices - Six Selected Sites, United States, 2014", CDC MMWR 66(15), April 2017 - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5687189/ (also https://cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/66/wr/mm6615a2.htm)
- Staff knowledge study: food allergy training was not associated with knowledge but with attitudes. Radke et al., "Food Allergy Knowledge and Attitudes of Restaurant Managers and Staff: An EHS-Net Study", CDC (year not confirmed, ~2016) - https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/44343/cdc_44343_DS1.pdf
- ED visits (older): ~1.9 million food-allergy ED visits 2001-2009; pediatric visits rose 77,000 (2001) to 92,000 (2009); adult fell 147,000 to 97,000. Weill Cornell news, 2013 - https://news.weill.cornell.edu/news/2013/12/research-highlight-food-allergy-emergency-visits-stable-among-children-declining-among-adults
- Anaphylaxis ED visits (all causes) 405,145 US visits 2008-2016, incidence up ~2.3-fold. Via snacksafely summary, 2019 - https://snacksafely.com/2019/08/study-emergency-department-visits-for-anaphylaxis-have-skyrocketed-since-2008/ (primary paper not fetched). No current CDC annual national food-allergy ED count found.
- Eating-out anxiety (UK, ages 16-24, n=2,599): 60% (FSA summary; another source says 64%) avoided eating out in past 6 months due to allergy; only 14% "extremely confident" asking for info; 59% tend to visit the same place. Food Standards Agency / Allergy UK / Anaphylaxis Campaign survey, year NOT CONFIRMED - https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/document/fsa_wave-1-report-v8-final-002.pdf ; https://www.djsresearch.co.uk/FoodMarketResearchInsightsAndFindings/article/Fear-caused-by-food-allergies-stopping-young-people-from-eating-out-survey-reveals-04238 . UK, not US; young people only.
- Adult anxiety (qualitative, 22 adults, UK, 2025): avoidance main coping strategy; many lack support. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11250200/ (via search; title not confirmed)
- Travel: interviews with 32 adults with peanut/tree nut allergy found foreign travel hard; coping included carrying allergy cards in host language. Barnett et al., Clinical and Translational Allergy, 2012 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3480958 (via search). Authors/title from summary, not fetched. No large-scale data on language barriers causing reactions found.
- FARE tip: use translated chef cards; advisors prefer human translation over Google Translate (advocate advice, anecdotal) - https://www.foodallergy.org/fare-blog/managing-food-allergies-abroad (not fetched).
